Invitation text message: 20 invites and RSVP texts to copy

A good invitation text message gives five facts in one or two lines: what the event is, when, where, who is invited and the date to reply by. Below are 20 invitation and RSVP texts for birthdays, parties, dinners and weddings that you can copy, plus how to send one to every guest as a separate message.

What should an invitation text message include?

An invitation text message should name the event, the date and start time, the place, who is invited and the date to reply by. Put the date and time in the first line, where they show in the notification preview. Add one helpful extra, such as parking, dress code or what to bring, and leave the rest for a link.

In the texts below, {first_name} is a placeholder for the guest's name, and anything in square brackets, such as [date] or [address], is yours to fill in. A clear reply-by date gets more answers than "let me know", because it gives people a reason to answer now.

Birthday party invitation texts

A birthday invitation text says whose birthday it is, when and where the party starts, and when to reply. For a kids' party, add the end time and whether parents should stay. Send it two to three weeks ahead and ask for an answer about a week before, so you can plan food and seats.

  1. Hi {first_name}! [Name] is turning [age] and we're celebrating on [date] at [time], [place]. Can you come? Please reply by [date].
  2. {first_name}, you're invited to my birthday dinner on [date] at [time] at [restaurant]. Let me know by [date] so I can book the table!
  3. Hi {first_name}, [child's name] would love for [guest's child] to come to [his/her/their] birthday party on [date], [time] to [time], at [place]. Please reply by [date].
  4. Surprise party alert! We're celebrating [name]'s birthday on [date] at [time], [place]. Please keep it secret and reply to me by [date].
  5. {first_name}, I'm turning [age]! Drinks at [place] on [date] from [time]. Come by any time. Reply so I know to expect you.

Party, dinner and holiday invitation texts

A party or dinner invitation text can be casual, but it still needs the date, time and address. Mention what you are serving or what guests should bring, and give a reply-by date a few days ahead. In December people's calendars fill fast, so send holiday party invitations three to four weeks before.

  1. Hi {first_name}! Dinner at my place on [date] at [time], [address]. Can you make it? Let me know by [date] and tell me if you have any food allergies.
  2. {first_name}, we're having a few friends over on [date] from [time]. Bring a drink if you like. Reply by [date] so I know how many to expect.
  3. You're invited to our holiday party on [date] at [time], [place]! Festive sweaters welcome. Please reply by [date], {first_name}.
  4. Hi {first_name}, game night at [place] on [date] at [time]. Reply YES or NO by [date] and I'll save you a seat.
  5. {first_name}, we're hosting a potluck on [date] at [time], [address]. Reply by [date] with what you'd like to bring.

Wedding, shower and formal invitation texts

For a wedding, baby shower or other formal event, a text usually backs up a paper invitation or links to an online one. Keep it warm and short, use full names, and point to the RSVP link or the reply date printed on the invitation. Send it once the invitations go out, not before.

  1. Hi {first_name}, [name] and [name] are getting married on [date] in [city]! Your invitation is here: [link]. Please RSVP by [date].
  2. {first_name}, you're invited to a baby shower for [name] on [date] at [time], [place]. Please reply by [date] so we can plan.
  3. Hi {first_name}, we'd be honored to have you at [event] on [date] at [time], [venue]. Details and RSVP: [link]
  4. {first_name}, the save the date for our wedding is on its way, but here's the short version: [date], [city]. Formal invitation to follow!
  5. Hi {first_name}, [name]'s retirement party is on [date] at [time] at [place]. Please let [name] know by [date] if you can join us.

RSVP text messages: reminders and follow-ups

An RSVP text message reminds guests of the deadline and makes answering easy. Send the first reminder two or three days before the deadline, and ask a yes-or-no question so a one-word reply is enough. After the deadline, text only the guests who haven't answered, and send everyone a short reminder the day before the event.

  1. Hi {first_name}, just checking: can you make it to [event] on [date]? Please reply YES or NO by [date].
  2. {first_name}, I'm finalizing numbers for [event] on [date]. Could you let me know today if you're coming?
  3. Hi {first_name}, quick one: will you be bringing a plus-one to [event]? Reply by [date] so I can plan seating.
  4. {first_name}, see you tomorrow at [time] for [event]! The address is [address]. Parking is [where].
  5. Thanks for letting me know, {first_name}! We'll miss you at [event]. I'll send photos afterwards.

Questions

Is it OK to send an invitation by text?

Yes, for most parties, dinners and kids' birthdays. For a wedding or a formal event, many hosts still send a paper or online invitation and use a text as a reminder or to share the RSVP link.

How far in advance should I text an invitation?

Two to three weeks for a birthday party, one to two weeks for a casual dinner, and three to four weeks for a holiday party in December. Send a reminder the day before.

How do I ask guests to RSVP by text?

Give a clear date and a simple question, such as "Please reply YES or NO by Friday." Two or three days before the deadline, text only the guests who haven't answered.

How do I send an invitation to many people without a group text?

Send each guest a separate message, either one by one or with a mass text app. Replies then come back to your private conversation with each guest.
